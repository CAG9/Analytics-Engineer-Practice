# Analytics Engineer Practice

A collection of small projects, exercises, and experiments that I use to practice and keep improving my analytics engineering skills.

This repository is intentionally focused on simple and practical projects. I use it as a space to try things out, revisit concepts, experiment with different approaches, and build consistency through hands-on practice.

Note: This repository is not intended to represent the full extent of my knowledge or experience. My more complex and production-oriented projects are maintained in separate repositories.

## Purpose

The main goals of this repository are to:

Practice analytics engineering concepts regularly.
Experiment with different tools, techniques, and workflows.
Turn concepts into small, practical projects.
Keep a record of things I have worked on and learned.
Explore ideas without the pressure of building a large production-ready project.
Continue learning in my free time.

## What You May Find Here

Projects and exercises may include topics such as:

* SQL
* Data transformation
* Data modeling
* dbt
* Python
* Data quality checks
* ETL / ELT concepts
* Analytics workflows
* Testing and documentation
* Small data projects
* Experiments with different data tools

The projects vary in scope and complexity. Some may be very small exercises, while others may evolve into more complete projects.

## Repository Philosophy

This repository is a practice space, not a portfolio of my most advanced work.

I prefer keeping small experiments here instead of over-engineering them. The goal is to learn, practice, and improve rather than make every project production-ready.

For more complex projects that require deeper architecture, larger datasets, production-style workflows, or more extensive engineering, I maintain separate repositories.

## Learning Through Practice

I believe that consistent practice is an important part of becoming a better engineer.

Some projects here may contain simple solutions, alternative approaches, unfinished ideas, or experiments. That's intentional. They represent the process of learning and reinforcing concepts through implementation.

As I learn new techniques, I may revisit older projects and improve them.

## Why This Repository Exists

I maintain this repository in my free time as a way to stay hands-on with data and analytics engineering.

It is a place where I can:

Learn → Build → Experiment → Review → Improve

If you're reviewing this repository as part of evaluating my work, I hope it gives you a sense of how I approach continuous learning and how I use small projects to keep my skills sharp.
